This is my Project 2 Portfolio Mandate and Stress-Test Decision System. My committee recommendation is pilot, not adopt. The current investable allocation is zero until the mandate owner approves the forward-looking return objective, the BIL reserve definition, implementation costs, and robustness criteria.

The app uses the committed monthly adjusted-price sample for BIL, IEF, LQD, and VT from November 2021 through October 2026. The active mandate is a 6.0% net annualized historical-return objective, a 10% minimum BIL reserve weight, and a 0.0% annual implementation-cost assumption. The search is long only and evaluates allocations in 5% increments.

With the default inputs, the constrained search selects 70% BIL and 30% VT, with 0% IEF and 0% LQD. Its net annualized historical-return estimate is 6.07%, annualized volatility is 4.54%, and BIL weight is 70%. The result passes the classroom historical-return and reserve rules, but it is not a forecast or an authorization to trade.

Point to the base-case portfolio comparison table. It shows equal weight, inverse volatility, and the constrained search. The first two are baselines, not recommendations. Equal weight spreads capital across the four assets. Inverse volatility weights lower-volatility assets more heavily. Under this historical sample, neither baseline clears the active 6.0% net return objective. The constrained result is selected because it is the lowest-volatility allocation that meets both active constraints.

Point to the risk-and-return chart. The horizontal axis is annualized volatility and the vertical axis is net annualized historical return. This visual shows the trade-off rather than hiding it in a single headline number. Then point to the allocation-composition chart and the active-allocation table. The app makes the allocation, return estimate, volatility, and BIL reserve weight visible together so a committee can see what rule is driving the decision.

Scroll to the pre-specified stress-scenarios table. These are named causal input changes, not vague optimistic and pessimistic labels. The table tests a one-point reduction in the VT return estimate, a last-36-month return window, a 0.50-point annual-cost increase, and a higher BIL reserve floor. A feasible result means only that the stated historical rules remain feasible; it does not change the recommendation to adopt.

The most important sensitivity is the return window. When the sample changes to the most recent 36 monthly returns, the selected allocation becomes 85% BIL, 5% LQD, and 10% VT. The largest sleeve change from the full sample is 20 percentage points. That is why the app’s conclusion is intentionally cautious.

The decision remains pilot, not adopt because the result is sensitive to the estimation window and because costs, liquidity, taxes, rebalancing, and forward performance are not modeled. I review the pilot at every month-end and return it to committee review within five business days if a stress has no feasible allocation, a sleeve changes by 20 percentage points or more across approved windows, BIL falls below the approved floor, or point-in-time data control fails.
