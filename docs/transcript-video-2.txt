The architecture has four layers. Now we are going to move on to this CSV file. `Lab_15_prices.csv` is the fixed monthly-price input, and `ALFRED_CPIAUCSL_vintages.csv` is the dated CPI monitoring input. Second, the `Lab_15_portfolio.py` file validates the price table, creates monthly returns, and estimates annualized arithmetic returns, volatility, and correlations.

Third, `lowest_risk_mix`, which I am going to pull up right here, enumerates every long-only allocation in 5% steps that sum to 100%, discards a candidate if it misses the return objective or BIL floor, and retains the feasible candidate with the lowest volatility. Now we will move on to the `app.py` file. It passes the active controls to that engine and displays baselines, the constrained results, stresses, and the CPI availability gate.

The financial logic is intentional. Equal weight and inverse volatility are transparent baselines. The constrained search does not maximize return. It applies the return and reserve rules first, then minimizes volatility among the feasible candidates. Costs are explicit. The app converts the selected gross historical estimate into a net estimate by subtracting the displayed annual-cost assumption. CPI is not used to select weights because the project has no defensible causal model linking one CPI observation to allocation weights. It is instead a point-in-time monitoring control.
